About me

Hi! I'm Joey Zobel (/ˈdʒoʊi ˈzoʊbɫ̩/) (he/him/his). I'm a sociophonetician and Ph.D. student in Linguistics at New York University. I'm mainly interested in the evolving status of regional variation in linguistic identity and its role in an increasingly mobile and diverse society.

Much of my past sociolinguistic work has focused on Appalachian and Southern English, but I'm also interested more broadly in North American English and other minority languages in the United States and Canada. In addition to English, I've done fieldwork in Georgian and phonetic work with French and Hindi.

When I'm not studying languages, you can find me playing classical piano, singing in choir, listening to Taylor Swift, and/or reading literary fiction.

Education & Background

I received a B.A. in Linguistics from Pomona College in 2025, where I minored in Mathematics and Late Antique–Medieval Studies. At Pomona, I worked with Ernesto R. Gutiérrez Topete, Mary Paster, Nicole R. Holliday, and Ken Wolf, and my senior project was a sociolinguistic analysis of the use of y'all in online discourse.

In 2025, I was a summer intern at Harvard University's Lab for Developmental Studies, where I worked on language acquisition with Elika Bergelson and Jesse Snedeker.
